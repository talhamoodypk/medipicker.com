All Day Slimming Tea (UK) Ingredients

The official website names 6 key ingredients in All Day Slimming Tea (UK). Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Oolong Tea

Oolong is a partly fermented tea containing caffeine and polyphenols. Small studies show slight increases in energy expenditure; weight-loss effects are small.

Ginseng

Ginseng (Panax) root is a traditional energy and vitality herb. Some trials show small improvements in fatigue and erectile-function scores in people; evidence quality is mixed.

Garcinia Cambogia (HCA)

Garcinia cambogia fruit rind contains hydroxycitric acid (HCA). A randomized trial in JAMA found no significant weight loss compared with placebo; meta-analyses show at most a tiny effect.

Ginger

Ginger root is a common spice and traditional remedy for nausea. It has good evidence for pregnancy and travel nausea and modest effects on inflammation; no direct evidence for skin conditions.

Dandelion Root

Dandelion root and leaf are traditional digestive and diuretic herbs. Human evidence is limited to a few small studies; dandelion leaf has a mild diuretic effect.

The page names these ingredients for the teas; the full blend and amounts aren't given in text. Its references also cite berberine, chromium, hesperidin and rutin studies, though those aren't in the tea ingredient list shown.

Is All Day Slimming Tea (UK) Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Some weight management formulas contain caffeine or green tea extract — avoid taking them late in the day if you're sensitive.
  • Talk to your doctor first if you take medication for diabetes, blood pressure or your heart.
  • Don't use weight-loss supplements while pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Ginseng can affect blood sugar and blood pressure and interacts with warfarin.
  • Garcinia has been linked to rare cases of liver injury.
