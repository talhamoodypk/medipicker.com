What Is Synadentix?

Synadentix Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“Order 6 Bottles and Get 3 FREE Bonuses”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Hydroxyapatite is a real, well-studied ingredient: in toothpaste it can remineralize early enamel lesions about as well as fluoride in some trials.
  • That benefit comes from direct contact with the teeth; a chewable may give brief contact, but swallowing it does nothing for enamel.
  • Lactoferrin has lab activity against gum bacteria, with only small human studies.
  • The claim that ingredients go 'directly into your bloodstream, cleansing and repairing' gums and teeth isn't supported.

Synadentix Ingredients

The official website names 3 key ingredients in Synadentix. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Microcrystalline Hydroxyapatite

Hydroxyapatite is the calcium-phosphate mineral that makes up tooth enamel and bone. In toothpaste, nano-hydroxyapatite remineralizes early enamel lesions and reduces sensitivity, about as well as fluoride in several trials. Swallowed, it's simply a calcium source.

Lactoferrin

Lactoferrin is an iron-binding protein found in milk and saliva. Lab studies show activity against gum-disease bacteria; human trials are small.

Beta-Glucanase (Enzyme)

Beta-glucanase is an enzyme that breaks down beta-glucans, a component of biofilms and plant fiber. It's studied in lab settings for breaking down dental biofilm; there's no clinical evidence in chewable supplements.

The website names these key ingredients in a proprietary mix of 'enzymes, plants and minerals' but doesn't give amounts. 'NASA-grade' is a marketing term; hydroxyapatite was studied by NASA decades ago for astronaut bone loss, which doesn't make a particular supplement 'NASA-grade'.
