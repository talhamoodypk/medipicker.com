What Is Lungora?

Lungora is sold as a once-daily capsule for respiratory support and healthy lung function in adults over 50. Its main sales page is styled as an 'advertorial' (an advert written to look like an independent review).

The ingredients it names (magnesium glycinate, alpha-lipoic acid, butcher's broom, L-carnitine, turmeric and CoQ10) are, in the page's own words, aimed at 'nerve health pathways' and circulation. This is a typical neuropathy formula, not a lung formula.

Lungora Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

Lungora Ingredients

The official website names 6 key ingredients in Lungora. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Magnesium

Magnesium is a mineral involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions, including muscle and nerve function and blood sugar control. Many adults get less than recommended. Supplements can help low magnesium levels, sleep quality in some studies and, in larger doses, constipation.

Alpha-Lipoic Acid

Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) is an antioxidant made in the body and used as a supplement for nerve pain. Trials in diabetic neuropathy show modest improvement in burning and tingling at 600 mg a day; it's prescribed for this in Germany.

Butcher's Broom

Butcher's broom root is used in Europe for varicose veins and leg heaviness. Some trials support it for chronic venous insufficiency symptoms; there's no evidence for lung health.

L-Carnitine

L-carnitine helps transport fatty acids into cells to be burned for energy. A 2016 meta-analysis found an average extra weight loss of about 1.3 kg compared with placebo, usually at 2 g a day or more.

Turmeric (Curcumin)

Turmeric's active compound, curcumin, is studied for inflammation and joint pain. Trials in people with knee arthritis show modest pain relief, comparable to some painkillers in a few studies; curcumin is poorly absorbed unless combined with fat or black pepper extract.

Coenzyme Q10

CoQ10 is an antioxidant involved in energy production in cells. It has some evidence for heart failure and may ease statin-related muscle aches in some people; there's no evidence for lung function.

Amounts are not disclosed.

Is Lungora Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor first if you take blood pressure, heart or blood-thinning medication.
  • See a doctor about chest pain, shortness of breath or a persistent cough.
  • Supplements don't replace prescribed treatment.
  • Alpha-lipoic acid can lower blood sugar; people with diabetes should monitor their levels.
  • Turmeric/curcumin supplements may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners and have been linked to rare liver injury.
  • CoQ10 may reduce the effect of warfarin.
