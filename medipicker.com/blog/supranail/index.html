What Is Supranail?

SupraNail is marketed as an 11-ingredient formula for healthy nails and feet, aimed at people with discolored or brittle toenails.

Its ingredient list reads like a traditional 'colon cleanse' blend: cascara sagrada bark (a stimulant laxative), prune, fennel, oat bran, licorice, cayenne and Senna auriculata. It contains no antifungal ingredient with evidence against nail fungus.

Supranail Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“Supports every aspect of nails & feet total wellbeing”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Toenail fungus (onychomycosis) is treated with antifungal medicines; none of SupraNail's ingredients has clinical evidence against it.
  • Cascara sagrada was removed from US over-the-counter laxatives by the FDA in 2002 because its safety and effectiveness weren't shown.
  • Vitamin C and E support collagen and skin in general, but don't treat nail infections.
  • The website calls the formula 'well known for maximum results' but cites no study.

Supranail Ingredients

The official website names 12 key ingredients in Supranail. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Senna Auriculata (Avaram)

Senna auriculata, also called Cassia auriculata or avaram, is a shrub from India and Sri Lanka used in Ayurveda, mainly for blood sugar. Research is mostly in animals; there are no human studies for nail or skin health.

Cascara Sagrada Bark

Cascara sagrada is a tree bark containing anthraquinones, a class of stimulant laxatives. It works as a laxative, but the FDA removed it from over-the-counter laxative products in 2002 because manufacturers didn't provide safety and effectiveness data. It has no known benefit for nails.

Oat Fiber

Oat fiber and oat bran supply beta-glucan, a soluble fiber. Oat beta-glucan has an approved heart-health claim for lowering cholesterol at 3 g a day, and it adds bulk to stool.

Licorice Root

Licorice root contains glycyrrhizin, which gives it its sweet taste. It is used for indigestion and coughs; there is no evidence for memory.

Pumpkin Seed Extract

Pumpkin seed provides zinc, plant sterols and healthy fats. Some trials suggest pumpkin seed extract helps urinary symptoms from an enlarged prostate; there's no evidence for nails or feet.

Cayenne Pepper

Cayenne contains capsaicin, the compound that makes chili peppers hot. Capsaicin slightly raises energy use and may reduce appetite in studies, but the effects are small; it can also irritate a sensitive stomach.

Fennel Seed

Fennel seed is a traditional remedy for gas and bloating. There is limited evidence for infant colic and digestion, and none for nail health.

Prune

Prunes (dried plums) are a traditional food remedy for constipation thanks to their fiber and sorbitol. Trials show whole prunes (about 50–100 g a day) improve stool frequency; the small amount in a capsule is unlikely to do the same.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Hops

Hops are the flowers used to flavor beer, traditionally used for sleep. Small studies combine hops with valerian for sleep; there's no evidence for nail or skin care.

Vitamin C

Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant vitamin. It is needed for collagen and immune function, but extra vitamin C has no proven effect on memory in people who eat fruit and vegetables.

Vitamin E

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin. The large SELECT trial found vitamin E supplements slightly increased prostate cancer risk in men, and it has shown no clear benefit for memory in healthy adults.

The website describes Senna auriculata as a holy plant 'used by Amazonian tribes', but it is actually native to India and Sri Lanka. Amounts for the 11-ingredient blend are not disclosed.

Is Supranail Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Do a patch test before using any new oil or cream on your skin.
  • See a doctor about persistent fungal infections, rashes or sudden hair loss.
  • Stop use if you notice irritation.
  • Stimulant laxatives such as cascara sagrada can cause cramps, diarrhea and loss of potassium, and shouldn't be used for more than a week without medical advice.
  • Licorice containing glycyrrhizin can raise blood pressure and lower potassium with regular use.
  • Cayenne/capsaicin can cause heartburn or stomach irritation in sensitive people.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
