What Is Prostadine?

Prostadine is a liquid dropper supplement sold to support a healthy prostate, kidneys and urinary tract. It contains nine ingredients, most of them seaweeds.

Four of the nine ingredients are iodine-rich seaweeds (nori, wakame, kelp and bladderwrack), with iodine added separately as well. The sales page cites studies on iodine and prostate cancer, but iodine supplements are not a recognized prostate treatment.

Prostadine Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“This Cold Drink Might Trigger Your Prostate”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Saw palmetto did no better than placebo for urinary symptoms in the largest trials (STEP, CAMUS).
  • Large amounts of iodine can trigger thyroid problems, especially in older adults with existing thyroid nodules.
  • Shilajit products have been found contaminated with heavy metals when not purified.
  • The 'cold drink' headline is a curiosity hook; there's no evidence a cold drink 'triggers' the prostate.

Prostadine Ingredients

The official website names 9 key ingredients in Prostadine. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Saw Palmetto

Saw palmetto berry extract is the most popular herbal remedy for an enlarged prostate (BPH). Early small trials looked promising, but the large, well-run STEP and CAMUS trials and a Cochrane review found it worked no better than placebo for urinary symptoms, even at triple doses.

Pomegranate Extract

Pomegranate is rich in antioxidant ellagitannins. Pomegranate juice was studied in men with rising PSA after prostate cancer treatment; a placebo-controlled trial found no significant benefit.

Shilajit

Shilajit is a tar-like mineral resin from mountain rocks used in Ayurveda. Small trials suggest it may raise testosterone and sperm count slightly.

Kelp

Kelp is a large seaweed used in supplements as a natural source of iodine. Iodine is essential for thyroid hormones, but most people in countries with iodized salt already get enough; extra iodine has no proven digestive benefit.

Bladderwrack

Bladderwrack is a brown seaweed that is naturally rich in iodine and alginate fiber. It is mainly a source of iodine; there is no good clinical evidence that it improves digestion.

Wakame

Wakame is an edible brown seaweed used in miso soup. It provides iodine and fucoxanthin; there are no prostate trials.

Nori Yaki

Nori is the red seaweed used to wrap sushi. It's a food source of iodine and protein; there's no evidence for prostate health.

Iodine

Iodine is a mineral the thyroid needs to make hormones. It is essential in small amounts (150 mcg a day for adults). Lab and observational studies on iodine and the prostate are preliminary.

Neem

Neem is an Indian tree whose leaves and oil are used in traditional medicine. Lab studies show antimicrobial activity; there's little human evidence for urinary health.

Amounts aren't disclosed, so the total iodine dose from four seaweeds plus added iodine is unknown.

Is Prostadine Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor before use if you take blood pressure, heart or prostate medication.
  • See a doctor about any new urinary symptoms — they can have causes that need medical care.
  • Don't exceed the recommended dose on the label.
  • Unpurified shilajit can contain heavy metals; buy only tested products.
  • Seaweeds such as kelp and bladderwrack contain iodine — people with thyroid conditions or on thyroid medication should check with a doctor first.
  • Too much iodine (above about 1,100 mcg a day) can cause thyroid problems; people with thyroid disease should avoid iodine and seaweed supplements unless a doctor advises them.
  • Neem oil should not be swallowed in large amounts and isn't safe for children or in pregnancy.
