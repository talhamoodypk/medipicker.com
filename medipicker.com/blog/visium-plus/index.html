What Is Visium Plus?

Visium Plus is sold as a natural formula to support the health of your eyes 'no matter if you are 40 or 80 years old'. The sales page is written as a letter from a creator who says he has researched plants for years.

When we read the ingredient list, it turned out to be a prostate and urinary formula: saw palmetto, pygeum, plant sterols, stinging nettle, uva ursi, buchu, gravel root, pumpkin seed and juniper. It contains none of the nutrients studied for eye health, such as lutein, zeaxanthin or the AREDS2 vitamin blend.

Visium Plus Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“One Simple Way To Maintain A Healthy Vision”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • The ingredient list matches a prostate supplement, not an eye supplement; we found no ingredient with evidence for vision.
  • The nutrients proven to slow age-related macular degeneration (the AREDS2 formula of vitamins C and E, zinc, copper, lutein and zeaxanthin) are not provided at studied doses.
  • Uva ursi is meant for short-term use only because of its hydroquinone content.
  • No study of Visium Plus is cited.

Visium Plus Ingredients

The official website names 11 key ingredients in Visium Plus. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Saw Palmetto

Saw palmetto berry extract is the most popular herbal remedy for an enlarged prostate (BPH). Early small trials looked promising, but the large, well-run STEP and CAMUS trials and a Cochrane review found it worked no better than placebo for urinary symptoms, even at triple doses.

Pygeum Africanum Bark

Pygeum is an extract of the African plum tree bark used in Europe for BPH. A Cochrane review of older, small trials found a moderate improvement in night-time urination and urine flow; high-quality modern trials are lacking.

Plant Sterol Complex (Beta-Sitosterol)

Plant sterols such as beta-sitosterol are cholesterol-like compounds found in nuts, seeds and vegetable oils. Beta-sitosterol improved urinary flow and symptom scores in a Cochrane review of four trials, though it did not shrink the prostate; sterols also modestly lower LDL cholesterol.

Stinging Nettle Root

Nettle root is used in Europe, often with saw palmetto, for prostate symptoms. Some trials suggest modest improvement in urinary symptoms, but study quality is mixed.

Uva Ursi

Uva ursi (bearberry) leaf contains arbutin, which turns into hydroquinone and is used for urinary infections. Evidence for preventing urinary infections is limited, and it has nothing to do with eye or prostate health.

Quercetin

Quercetin is a plant flavonoid found in onions, apples and tea. Small studies suggest possible benefits for chronic prostatitis pain and blood pressure, but evidence is limited.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Lycopene (Tomato Fruit Powder)

Lycopene is the red carotenoid in tomatoes. Diets high in tomato products are linked with lower prostate cancer risk in observational studies, but supplement trials have not shown a clear benefit for BPH symptoms.

Goldenseal

Goldenseal is a North American root containing berberine and hydrastine. It has little clinical evidence for any use and none for mental focus.

Vitamin E

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin. The large SELECT trial found vitamin E supplements slightly increased prostate cancer risk in men, and it has shown no clear benefit for memory in healthy adults.

Zinc

Zinc is an essential mineral found in high concentrations in the prostate. Supplements help if you are deficient, but there is no good evidence that extra zinc improves BPH symptoms; long-term high doses can cause copper deficiency.

The full list on the website also includes vitamin B6, copper, selenium, red raspberry, Annona muricata (graviola), cat's claw, broccoli leaf, maitake, reishi, shiitake, juniper, buchu, calcium D-glucarate, pumpkin, burdock, cayenne pepper, gravel root and marshmallow root, 29 ingredients in all, with no amounts.

Is Visium Plus Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Have regular eye exams and see an eye doctor about sudden vision changes.
  • Supplements don't replace glasses, contact lenses or prescribed treatment.
  • Check with your doctor if you take medication.
  • Uva ursi should only be used short term (no more than about a week at a time, a few times a year) because of hydroquinone; avoid in pregnancy and kidney disease.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Goldenseal blocks liver enzymes (CYP3A4 and CYP2D6) that clear many medicines, so it can raise drug levels; it is not recommended in pregnancy.
