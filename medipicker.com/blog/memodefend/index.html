What Is MemoDefend?

MemoDefend is a memory supplement sold with the story of a mother who 'came back from total memory loss'. The sales letter is written by a researcher 'near the end of his career' who shares memory tips.

Its named ingredients (hawthorn, garlic, olive leaf, hibiscus, buchu, juniper and green tea) are a typical blood pressure formula. Controlling blood pressure does help protect the brain over time, but that's done with lifestyle changes and medication, not this blend.

MemoDefend Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“My Mom "Came Back" From Total Memory Loss, And This Is How She Did It”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • No supplement can reverse 'total memory loss'; dementia can't be reversed, though some causes of memory problems (B12 deficiency, thyroid disease, depression, medication effects) can be treated.
  • The letter's tips (stay hydrated, limit alcohol, don't smoke) are sensible.
  • Hawthorn, garlic and hibiscus may modestly lower blood pressure, which is relevant to long-term brain health but isn't a memory treatment.
  • Juniper and buchu are diuretic herbs not suited to long-term use.

MemoDefend Ingredients

The official website names 9 key ingredients in MemoDefend. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Hawthorn Berry

Hawthorn is a traditional heart and circulation herb. Some trials suggest small benefits in mild heart failure symptoms; there's no direct evidence for erections.

Garlic

Garlic contains allicin and other sulfur compounds. Garlic supplements modestly lower blood pressure and cholesterol in trials; effects on blood sugar are small.

Olive Leaf Extract

Olive leaf extract is rich in the polyphenol oleuropein. Small studies suggest modest effects on blood pressure and blood sugar; there is no evidence for memory.

Hibiscus Sabdariffa

Hibiscus flower is used to make a tart red tea. Trials show hibiscus tea modestly lowers blood pressure; it has a mild diuretic effect.

Buchu

Buchu is a South African herb traditionally used as a diuretic and urinary antiseptic. There's no clinical evidence for memory or urinary health.

Juniper Berry

Juniper berries are used as a flavoring (in gin) and as a traditional diuretic. There is no clinical evidence for blood sugar, blood pressure or urinary health.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Vitamin C

Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant vitamin. It is needed for collagen and immune function, but extra vitamin C has no proven effect on memory in people who eat fruit and vegetables.

Niacin (Vitamin B3)

Niacin is a B vitamin used at high doses to lower cholesterol. Nicotinamide (a form of B3) is being studied for glaucoma; regular niacin has no proven vision benefit.

The letter names these 'and many more'; the full label and amounts aren't shown.

Is MemoDefend Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor if you take blood thinners, antidepressants or other medication.
  • See a doctor about sudden or worsening memory problems.
  • Some formulas contain caffeine — check the label if you're sensitive.
  • Hawthorn can add to the effect of heart and blood pressure medicines, including digoxin.
  • Garlic supplements can increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.
  • Hibiscus can add to the effect of blood pressure medicines.
  • Buchu can irritate the kidneys and isn't recommended in pregnancy.
  • Juniper isn't recommended in pregnancy or for people with kidney disease.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • High-dose niacin causes flushing and, rarely, liver problems and swelling of the retina.
