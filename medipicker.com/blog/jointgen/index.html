Jointgen Claims vs. Evidence

  • Mobilee is one of the few branded joint ingredients with its own placebo-controlled trials, which showed modest improvements in knee pain and function.
  • The '14 randomized trials' claim includes small and manufacturer-funded studies; independent evidence is more limited.

  • Ginger has modest evidence for arthritis pain.

Jointgen Ingredients

The official website names 3 key ingredients in Jointgen. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Mobilee (Hyaluronic Acid Matrix)

Mobilee is a branded extract of rooster comb containing hyaluronic acid, collagen and polysaccharides. Placebo-controlled trials at 80 mg a day showed modest improvements in mild knee pain and function; most studies were funded by the manufacturer.

French Maritime Pine Bark (Pycnogenol)

French maritime pine bark extract is rich in antioxidant procyanidins. Small trials suggest it reduces knee arthritis pain and stiffness; larger independent trials are lacking.

Ginger

Ginger root is a common spice and traditional remedy for nausea. It has good evidence for pregnancy and travel nausea and modest effects on inflammation; no direct evidence for skin conditions.

The page states an 80 mg dose of Mobilee, the amount used in its trials. Other amounts aren't given in text.

Is Jointgen Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • See a doctor about numbness, burning or weakness — these can be signs of conditions that need treatment.
  • Check with your doctor if you take blood thinners or diabetes medication.
  • Stop using the product if you notice any unusual reaction.
  • Mobilee comes from rooster comb, so it isn't suitable for people allergic to chicken or eggs, or for vegetarians.
