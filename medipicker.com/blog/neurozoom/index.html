What Is Neurozoom?

NeuroZoom is sold as a memory and cognitive-health supplement with a 'proprietary formula of 35 memory essential antioxidants, minerals, vitamins and herbs'. In practice it is a broad multivitamin with a few brain-related extras.

Neurozoom Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“People With Early Alzheimer's Do This Bathroom Mistake”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • The 'bathroom mistake' headline is a curiosity hook; there is no known bathroom habit that signals or causes Alzheimer's.
  • Huperzine A acts like some prescription Alzheimer's drugs, but a large US trial found no benefit for Alzheimer's disease.
  • Bacopa and phosphatidylserine have small studies showing modest memory benefits at doses a 35-ingredient blend is unlikely to reach.
  • Vitamins only help memory if you are deficient (B12 in particular).

Neurozoom Ingredients

The official website names 12 key ingredients in Neurozoom. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Huperzine A

Huperzine A is a compound from Chinese club moss that blocks the breakdown of acetylcholine, similar to some prescription Alzheimer's drugs. Chinese studies reported benefits, but a US National Institute on Aging trial found no improvement in Alzheimer's disease at standard doses.

Bacopa Monnieri

Bacopa is an Ayurvedic herb traditionally used for memory. Several randomized trials found modest improvements in memory recall after 8–12 weeks at about 300 mg of standardized extract a day; it can cause stomach upset.

Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine is a fat that forms part of brain cell membranes. The FDA allows only a qualified health claim for memory, noting that the evidence is very limited; most studies used 100 mg three times a day.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Olive Leaf Extract

Olive leaf extract is rich in the polyphenol oleuropein. Small studies suggest modest effects on blood pressure and blood sugar; there is no evidence for memory.

Bilberry

Bilberry is a European relative of the blueberry, rich in anthocyanins. It is mostly studied for eye health with mixed results; there is no good evidence for memory.

Licorice Root

Licorice root contains glycyrrhizin, which gives it its sweet taste. It is used for indigestion and coughs; there is no evidence for memory.

Chromium

Chromium is a trace mineral involved in insulin action. Meta-analyses show at most small effects on blood sugar and weight; deficiency is rare.

Vitamin E

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin. The large SELECT trial found vitamin E supplements slightly increased prostate cancer risk in men, and it has shown no clear benefit for memory in healthy adults.

Vitamin C

Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant vitamin. It is needed for collagen and immune function, but extra vitamin C has no proven effect on memory in people who eat fruit and vegetables.

Selenium

Selenium is a trace mineral needed for antioxidant enzymes and thyroid hormone metabolism. Supplements help people who are deficient, which is uncommon in the US; extra selenium has no proven digestive benefit.

Magnesium

Magnesium is a mineral involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions, including muscle and nerve function and blood sugar control. Many adults get less than recommended. Supplements can help low magnesium levels, sleep quality in some studies and, in larger doses, constipation.

The full list also includes riboflavin, niacin, vitamin B6, thiamine, biotin, choline, beta carotene, boron, zinc, vitamin B5, N-acetyl-L-tyrosine, DHA, vanadyl sulphate, calcium, cinnamon bark, grapefruit seed extract, molybdenum, inositol, iron, manganese and L-glutamine. With 35 ingredients in a few capsules, most can only be present in small amounts.

Is Neurozoom Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor if you take blood thinners, antidepressants or other medication.
  • See a doctor about sudden or worsening memory problems.
  • Some formulas contain caffeine — check the label if you're sensitive.
  • Huperzine A can slow the heart rate and interact with Alzheimer's medicines and anticholinergic drugs.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Licorice containing glycyrrhizin can raise blood pressure and lower potassium with regular use.
  • Selenium is toxic in high amounts — don't combine several supplements that contain it.
