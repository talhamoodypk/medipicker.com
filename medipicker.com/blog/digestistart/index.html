What Is DigestiStart?

DigestiStart is a once-a-day capsule sold for smooth digestion. The sales page says it helps 'flush out all the lead that has been coating your gut muscles for years' and restart digestion.

Its 11 ingredients are a traditional Chinese 'kidney tonic' formula (rehmannia, cornus, dioscorea/wild yam, poria, cistanche, dodder seed, eucommia and others), more often used in TCM for fatigue, low back pain and libido than for digestion.

DigestiStart Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“A Natural Breakthrough Solution That Supports A Smooth Digestion And A Healthy Gastrointestinal Tract”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Lead doesn't 'coat gut muscles'; lead poisoning is a serious medical condition diagnosed with a blood test and treated by doctors.
  • None of the 11 herbs has clinical evidence for digestion in this combination.
  • The '341,760 Americans' figure can't be verified.
  • Imported TCM herbs have sometimes been contaminated with heavy metals, ironically including lead.

DigestiStart Ingredients

The official website names 11 key ingredients in DigestiStart. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Rehmannia Root

Rehmannia is a core herb of traditional Chinese 'kidney tonic' formulas. Research is mostly in animals; there's no clinical evidence for digestion.

Cornus Fruit (Shan Zhu Yu)

Cornus (Asiatic dogwood) fruit is a traditional Chinese tonic herb. Evidence is limited to lab and animal studies.

Wild Yam (Dioscorea)

Wild yam root contains diosgenin, a plant compound used in labs to make hormones. The body can't turn wild yam into hormones, and there's no good evidence for digestion.

Poria Cocos

Poria is a fungus used in traditional Chinese medicine as a calming diuretic. Human evidence is minimal.

Cistanche

Cistanche is a desert plant used in TCM as a 'yang' tonic for fatigue and libido. Studies are mostly in animals.

Dodder Seed (Cuscuta)

Dodder seed is a TCM herb used for kidney and reproductive health. There's very little human evidence.

Eucommia Bark

Eucommia bark is used in TCM for back pain and blood pressure. A few small studies suggest a mild blood-pressure effect; nothing for digestion.

Achyranthes Root

Achyranthes is a TCM herb for joints and circulation. Evidence is mostly from animal studies.

Polygala Root

Polygala tenuifolia is used in TCM for memory and calm. Small studies suggest possible memory effects; it can cause stomach upset.

Schisandra Berry

Schisandra is an adaptogenic berry used in Chinese and Russian traditional medicine. Small studies suggest possible effects on fatigue and liver enzymes; evidence is limited.

Cnidium Monnieri

Cnidium seed contains osthole and is used in TCM for libido and skin itching. Evidence is mostly from lab and animal studies.

The label lists these ingredients in a proprietary blend without amounts.

Is DigestiStart Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Fiber and probiotic products can cause gas or bloating at first.
  • See a doctor about ongoing stomach pain, blood in the stool or unexplained weight loss.
  • Check with your doctor if you have a digestive condition or take medication.
  • Several traditional Chinese herbs, including achyranthes, are not recommended in pregnancy.
  • Schisandra can change how the liver processes some medicines (such as tacrolimus).

Our Verdict on DigestiStart

DigestiStart sells a traditional Chinese tonic with an invented explanation for digestive problems. If you have ongoing bloating, pain or changes in bowel habits, see a doctor; common causes like IBS, reflux, celiac disease and food intolerances are diagnosable and treatable. If you're worried about lead exposure, ask for a blood test.
