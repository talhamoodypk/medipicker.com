What Is UltraK9 Pro?

UltraK9 Pro is a liquid supplement for dogs, added to food, sold to support energy, digestion, joints, a healthy weight and a calmer temperament. It contains nine herbs and nutrients in an MCT oil base.

UltraK9 Pro Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“These Primal Nutrients Support A Healthy Happy Dog”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • None of these herbs has been studied for energy, weight or behavior in dogs; nearly all research is in people or lab animals.
  • Turmeric has some veterinary research for joint comfort, but curcumin is poorly absorbed without help (the MCT oil base may help slightly).
  • Horsetail contains thiaminase, an enzyme that destroys vitamin B1 and has caused poisoning in grazing animals.
  • Ashwagandha and ginseng can affect thyroid function, blood sugar and blood pressure; their safety in dogs is not established.

UltraK9 Pro Ingredients

The official website names 8 key ingredients in UltraK9 Pro. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Burdock Root

Burdock root is a traditional 'blood purifier' herb and a vegetable in Japan. Human evidence is very limited; most claims come from traditional use and lab studies.

Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an Ayurvedic herb classed as an adaptogen. Several small randomized trials found it lowered perceived stress and cortisol and improved sleep at 250–600 mg of root extract a day.

Ginseng

Ginseng (Panax) root is a traditional energy and vitality herb. Some trials show small improvements in fatigue and erectile-function scores in people; evidence quality is mixed.

Astragalus Root

Astragalus is a root used in traditional Chinese medicine to support immunity. Research is mostly in cells and animals, with very few studies in dogs.

Dandelion Root

Dandelion root and leaf are traditional digestive and diuretic herbs. Human evidence is limited to a few small studies; dandelion leaf has a mild diuretic effect.

Turmeric (Curcumin)

Turmeric's active compound, curcumin, is studied for inflammation and joint pain. Trials in people with knee arthritis show modest pain relief, comparable to some painkillers in a few studies; curcumin is poorly absorbed unless combined with fat or black pepper extract.

MCT Oil

MCT oil is a fat made of medium-chain triglycerides, usually from coconut. It is easily digested and used as a carrier; studies in dogs have looked at it for epilepsy and cognitive aging, not for joints.

Horsetail

Horsetail (Equisetum) is a plant rich in silica. There's little evidence it improves bone or joint health.

The website lists nine ingredients in a proprietary formula with no amounts and no dosing by body weight, which is unusual for a pet product. Its ingredient list differs in places from other pages that mention bovine collagen.

Is UltraK9 Pro Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Ask your vet before giving any supplement, especially if your dog takes medication or is pregnant.
  • Follow the dosing for your dog's weight.
  • See your vet about any sudden change in appetite, energy or behavior.
  • Ashwagandha isn't recommended in pregnancy, with thyroid medication or for people with autoimmune conditions; rare cases of liver injury have been reported.
  • Ginseng can affect blood sugar and blood pressure and interacts with warfarin.
  • Turmeric/curcumin supplements may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners and have been linked to rare liver injury.
  • Horsetail contains thiaminase, which breaks down vitamin B1; it has poisoned grazing animals and isn't recommended for long-term use.
