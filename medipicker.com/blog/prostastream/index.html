What Is ProstaStream?

ProstaStream is a prostate supplement sold to men with frequent night-time urination and a weak stream. It combines saw palmetto, pygeum, plant sterols and a trio of Japanese mushrooms with zinc, selenium and vitamins.

ProstaStream Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“15-Second Male Bedtime Ritual Shrinks Your Prostate in Just Weeks”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • No supplement can 'shrink your prostate in weeks'. Prescription drugs for enlarged prostate (BPH) take months to reduce prostate size.
  • Saw palmetto has been tested in large, well-run trials (STEP and CAMUS) and worked no better than placebo for urinary symptoms.
  • Pygeum and beta-sitosterol (plant sterols) have modest supporting evidence from older, smaller trials.
  • The mushroom, broccoli and green tea extracts have no clinical evidence for prostate symptoms.

ProstaStream Ingredients

The official website names 15 key ingredients in ProstaStream. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Saw Palmetto

Saw palmetto berry extract is the most popular herbal remedy for an enlarged prostate (BPH). Early small trials looked promising, but the large, well-run STEP and CAMUS trials and a Cochrane review found it worked no better than placebo for urinary symptoms, even at triple doses.

Pygeum Africanum Bark

Pygeum is an extract of the African plum tree bark used in Europe for BPH. A Cochrane review of older, small trials found a moderate improvement in night-time urination and urine flow; high-quality modern trials are lacking.

Plant Sterol Complex (Beta-Sitosterol)

Plant sterols such as beta-sitosterol are cholesterol-like compounds found in nuts, seeds and vegetable oils. Beta-sitosterol improved urinary flow and symptom scores in a Cochrane review of four trials, though it did not shrink the prostate; sterols also modestly lower LDL cholesterol.

Maitake Mushroom

Maitake (Grifola frondosa) is an edible mushroom rich in beta-glucans. Research is mostly in cells and animals; there is no clinical evidence for prostate health.

Reishi Mushroom

Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum) is a mushroom used in traditional Chinese medicine. One small Japanese trial found a modest improvement in urinary symptom scores; larger studies are needed.

Shiitake Mushroom

Shiitake (Lentinula edodes) is a common edible mushroom. It provides beta-glucans that interact with the immune system, but there are no clinical studies for prostate symptoms.

Cat's Claw

Cat's claw (Uncaria tomentosa) is a vine bark from the Amazon used for inflammation. Small studies explored it for arthritis; there is no evidence for prostate health.

Lycopene (Tomato Fruit Powder)

Lycopene is the red carotenoid in tomatoes. Diets high in tomato products are linked with lower prostate cancer risk in observational studies, but supplement trials have not shown a clear benefit for BPH symptoms.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Broccoli Leaf Extract

Broccoli extracts provide glucosinolates that the body converts into sulforaphane. Sulforaphane is being studied for cancer prevention, but there are no trials showing benefits for prostate symptoms.

Selenium

Selenium is a trace mineral needed for antioxidant enzymes and thyroid hormone metabolism. Supplements help people who are deficient, which is uncommon in the US; extra selenium has no proven digestive benefit.

Vitamin E

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin. The large SELECT trial found vitamin E supplements slightly increased prostate cancer risk in men, and it has shown no clear benefit for memory in healthy adults.

Vitamin B6

Vitamin B6 is involved in protein metabolism and making neurotransmitters. Most people get enough from food; very high doses over time can cause nerve damage.

Zinc

Zinc is an essential mineral found in high concentrations in the prostate. Supplements help if you are deficient, but there is no good evidence that extra zinc improves BPH symptoms; long-term high doses can cause copper deficiency.

Copper

Copper is an essential trace mineral involved in iron metabolism and connective tissue. Deficiency is rare; there's no evidence extra copper helps digestion.

The video transcript names these ingredients but gives no amounts. Tomato fruit powder is the source of lycopene, and broccoli leaf extract is listed for its sulforaphane-type compounds.

Is ProstaStream Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor before use if you take blood pressure, heart or prostate medication.
  • See a doctor about any new urinary symptoms — they can have causes that need medical care.
  • Don't exceed the recommended dose on the label.
  • Reishi may increase bleeding risk and has been linked to rare liver problems.
  • Cat's claw may affect blood pressure and blood clotting and isn't recommended for people with autoimmune disease.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Selenium is toxic in high amounts — don't combine several supplements that contain it.
