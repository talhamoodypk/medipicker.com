What Is Artivorin?

Artivorin is a daily joint supplement for comfort, flexibility and mobility. Its main page is an advertorial titled like a consumer review ('Reviews, Consumer Reports, Complaints').

Its key ingredients (turmeric, CoQ10, butcher's broom and alpha-lipoic acid) are the same core formula used by Lungora, which is sold for lung health.

Artivorin Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Curcumin (95% extract) has the best evidence here, with modest pain relief in knee osteoarthritis trials.

Artivorin Ingredients

The official website names 4 key ingredients in Artivorin. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Turmeric (Curcumin)

Turmeric's active compound, curcumin, is studied for inflammation and joint pain. Trials in people with knee arthritis show modest pain relief, comparable to some painkillers in a few studies; curcumin is poorly absorbed unless combined with fat or black pepper extract.

Coenzyme Q10

CoQ10 is an antioxidant involved in energy production in cells. It has some evidence for heart failure and may ease statin-related muscle aches in some people; there's no evidence for lung function.

Butcher's Broom

Butcher's broom root is used in Europe for varicose veins and leg heaviness. Some trials support it for chronic venous insufficiency symptoms; there's no evidence for lung health.

Alpha-Lipoic Acid

Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) is an antioxidant made in the body and used as a supplement for nerve pain. Trials in diabetic neuropathy show modest improvement in burning and tingling at 600 mg a day; it's prescribed for this in Germany.

Turmeric is standardized to 95% curcuminoids, a common, studied extract. Amounts per capsule aren't disclosed.

Is Artivorin Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor before use if you take medication, are pregnant or nursing, or have a health condition.
  • Don't exceed the recommended dose on the label.
  • Stop use if you notice any unusual reaction.
  • Turmeric/curcumin supplements may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners and have been linked to rare liver injury.
  • CoQ10 may reduce the effect of warfarin.
  • Alpha-lipoic acid can lower blood sugar; people with diabetes should monitor their levels.
