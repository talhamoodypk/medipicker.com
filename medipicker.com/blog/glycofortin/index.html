What Is GlycoFortin?

GlycoFortin is a liquid dropper supplement sold to support blood sugar by supporting the pancreas and liver.

Its ingredient list is almost identical to typical weight-loss drops: guarana, green tea, raspberry ketones, African mango, forskolin, grapefruit and capsicum, alongside chromium and gymnema. It's a fat-burner formula with a blood sugar label.

GlycoFortin Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“The Unique Blood Sugar Solution That Supports The Health Of Your Pancreas & Liver”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Only chromium and gymnema have any blood sugar evidence, and their effects are small.
  • Guarana and green tea add caffeine; caffeine can temporarily raise blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes.
  • Raspberry ketones and forskolin have no meaningful human evidence.
  • There's no evidence the formula 'supports the pancreas'.

GlycoFortin Ingredients

The official website names 13 key ingredients in GlycoFortin. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Chromium

Chromium is a trace mineral involved in insulin action. Meta-analyses show at most small effects on blood sugar and weight; deficiency is rare.

Gymnema Sylvestre

Gymnema is an Ayurvedic herb known as 'sugar destroyer' because it blocks the taste of sweetness. Small trials suggest modest reductions in blood sugar in people with diabetes; quality is low.

Maca Root

Maca is a Peruvian root vegetable sold for energy and libido. Small trials suggest it may improve libido slightly; there is no evidence for hearing.

Grape Seed Extract

Grape seed extract is rich in antioxidant proanthocyanidins. It may modestly lower blood pressure; protection against hearing loss has only been shown in animal studies.

Guarana

Guarana seed is a natural source of caffeine. Its caffeine slightly raises alertness and energy use; weight-loss effects are small.

African Mango (Irvingia gabonensis)

African mango seed extract is sold for appetite and weight control. A few small trials reported weight loss, but systematic reviews rate the evidence as low quality.

Eleuthero (Siberian Ginseng)

Eleuthero is an adaptogenic root used for stamina and stress. Studies are mixed and mostly old; there's no reliable evidence for weight loss or blood sugar.

Astragalus Root

Astragalus is a root used in traditional Chinese medicine to support immunity. Research is mostly in cells and animals, with very few studies in dogs.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Forskolin (Coleus forskohlii)

Forskolin is a compound from the coleus plant that raises cAMP in cells. Two small trials showed mixed results on body fat and no clear weight loss.

Capsicum Annuum (Chili Pepper)

Capsicum is chili pepper extract, a source of capsaicin. Capsaicin has small effects on metabolism and appetite; there's no evidence it helps hearing.

Raspberry Ketones

Raspberry ketone is the aroma compound in raspberries, popularized by TV shows as a fat burner. There are no good human trials showing it causes weight loss on its own.

Ginseng

Ginseng (Panax) root is a traditional energy and vitality herb. Some trials show small improvements in fatigue and erectile-function scores in people; evidence quality is mixed.

The list also includes grapefruit fruit extract. Amounts are not disclosed.

Is GlycoFortin Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • If you have diabetes or take insulin or glucose-lowering medicine, speak to your doctor first — some ingredients can lower blood sugar further.
  • Keep monitoring your blood sugar as advised by your doctor; supplements don't replace prescribed treatment.
  • Avoid during pregnancy or breastfeeding unless your doctor approves.
  • Gymnema can lower blood sugar and may add to the effect of diabetes medicine.
  • Caffeine sources such as guarana and green tea can cause jitters, poor sleep and a faster heart rate; count them alongside coffee and energy drinks.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Ginseng can affect blood sugar and blood pressure and interacts with warfarin.

Our Verdict on GlycoFortin

GlycoFortin is effectively a fat-burner drop sold to people worried about blood sugar. Its caffeine sources may even work against that goal. For blood sugar, rely on proven steps and your doctor's advice, and check with them before taking any herbal product alongside diabetes medicine.
