Brain Memory Keeper Ingredients

The official website names 7 key ingredients in Brain Memory Keeper. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Alpha-GPC

Alpha-GPC (choline alfoscerate) is a choline compound that the brain uses to make acetylcholine, a messenger involved in memory. It is prescribed in some European countries for cognitive decline, and a few clinical trials in people with Alzheimer's or vascular dementia showed small benefits; evidence in healthy adults is limited.

Ginkgo Biloba

Ginkgo leaf extract, usually standardized to 24% flavone glycosides, is one of the most-studied herbal memory supplements. Results are mixed: some trials show small benefits in people with dementia, but the large GEM trial of over 3,000 older adults found it did not prevent dementia or cognitive decline.

Bacopa Monnieri

Bacopa is an Ayurvedic herb traditionally used for memory. Several randomized trials found modest improvements in memory recall after 8–12 weeks at about 300 mg of standardized extract a day; it can cause stomach upset.

Acetyl-L-Carnitine

Acetyl-L-carnitine is a form of the amino acid carnitine that crosses into the brain. Some trials in older adults with mild cognitive impairment showed small benefits, usually at 1.5–3 g a day.

L-Glutamine

L-glutamine is the most abundant amino acid in the body and a fuel for gut cells. There is no good evidence that supplemental glutamine improves memory in healthy people.

Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine is a fat that forms part of brain cell membranes. The FDA allows only a qualified health claim for memory, noting that the evidence is very limited; most studies used 100 mg three times a day.

St. John's Wort

St. John's Wort is an herb studied mainly for mild to moderate depression. It has reasonable evidence for mild depression, but none for memory or focus.

The website lists ginkgo leaf extract standardized to 24% (the same standard used in most ginkgo studies) and St. John's Wort at 0.3% hypericin, but does not give milligram amounts for the blend.

Is Brain Memory Keeper Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor if you take blood thinners, antidepressants or other medication.
  • See a doctor about sudden or worsening memory problems.
  • Some formulas contain caffeine — check the label if you're sensitive.
  • Ginkgo may increase bleeding risk; avoid combining it with blood thinners or aspirin and stop it before surgery.
  • St. John's Wort interacts with many medicines, including antidepressants, birth control pills, blood thinners, heart and HIV drugs and transplant medicines; it can make them less effective or cause serotonin syndrome.
