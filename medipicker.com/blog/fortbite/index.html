What Is Fortbite?

FortBite is a tooth powder you put on your toothbrush twice a day instead of, or as well as, toothpaste. It's based on two clays (bentonite and kaolin) with calcium carbonate and herbal flavors.

Fortbite Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“This Ancient Volcanic Clay Supports Healthy Gums And Teeth”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Calcium carbonate is a mild abrasive used in many toothpastes; it helps remove stains and plaque but doesn't rebuild enamel.
  • Clay 'pulling toxins out' of teeth isn't a recognized dental concept.
  • Without fluoride, a tooth powder offers less cavity protection than standard toothpaste.
  • The page discourages professional cleanings as 'expensive and painful', but they remove hardened tartar that brushing can't.

Fortbite Ingredients

The official website names 8 key ingredients in Fortbite. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Bentonite Clay

Bentonite is a volcanic clay sold as a 'detox' ingredient because it binds substances in the gut. There is little human research supporting it for digestion or detox, and clay can also bind nutrients and medicines.

Kaolin Clay

Kaolin is a soft white clay used in cosmetics and some old diarrhea remedies. It's a mild abrasive and absorbent; there's no evidence it strengthens teeth.

Calcium Carbonate

Calcium carbonate (chalk) is a mild abrasive used in many toothpastes. It helps remove plaque and surface stains; it doesn't remineralize enamel the way fluoride or hydroxyapatite can.

Cinnamon

Cinnamon bark (usually cassia) is a common spice studied for blood sugar. Meta-analyses show small, inconsistent reductions in fasting blood sugar; it doesn't meaningfully lower HbA1c.

Clove Bud Oil

Clove oil is rich in eugenol, a numbing and antiseptic compound. It shows antifungal activity in lab tests; clinical evidence on nails is lacking.

Peppermint Oil

Peppermint oil relaxes the smooth muscle of the gut. Enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules have good evidence for irritable bowel syndrome symptoms; there's no evidence for blood sugar.

Myrrh

Myrrh is a resin from Commiphora trees used in traditional medicine and mouthwashes. There is no clinical evidence that myrrh improves focus or mental clarity.

Ginger

Ginger root is a common spice and traditional remedy for nausea. It has good evidence for pregnancy and travel nausea and modest effects on inflammation; no direct evidence for skin conditions.

It also contains lemon peel and stevia for taste. FortBite does not list fluoride or hydroxyapatite, the two ingredients with the best evidence for preventing cavities.

Is Fortbite Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Keep brushing, flossing and visiting your dentist — supplements don't replace dental care.
  • See a dentist about tooth pain, bleeding gums or swelling.
  • Products containing xylitol must be kept away from dogs.
  • Clay ingredients such as bentonite can bind medicines — take them at least two hours apart from any prescription.
  • Cassia cinnamon contains coumarin, which can harm the liver in large regular amounts.
  • Peppermint oil can worsen heartburn and reflux.

Our Verdict on Fortbite

FortBite is a basic clay tooth powder. If you use it, keep a fluoride or hydroxyapatite toothpaste in your routine for cavity protection, and keep up regular dental check-ups and cleanings. Bleeding gums are a sign of gum disease that a dentist can treat.
