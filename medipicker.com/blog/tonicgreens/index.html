TonicGreens Ingredients

The official website names 6 key ingredients in TonicGreens. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Spirulina

Spirulina is a blue-green alga sold as a protein- and nutrient-rich 'superfood'. Small studies suggest modest effects on cholesterol; evidence for immunity is limited.

Turmeric (Curcumin)

Turmeric's active compound, curcumin, is studied for inflammation and joint pain. Trials in people with knee arthritis show modest pain relief, comparable to some painkillers in a few studies; curcumin is poorly absorbed unless combined with fat or black pepper extract.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Quercetin

Quercetin is a plant flavonoid found in onions, apples and tea. Small studies suggest possible benefits for chronic prostatitis pain and blood pressure, but evidence is limited.

Resveratrol

Resveratrol is a polyphenol in grapes and berries, made famous by 'red wine' longevity research. Human trials have been disappointing, with small or no effects on heart health, blood sugar and aging.

Cinnamon

Cinnamon bark (usually cassia) is a common spice studied for blood sugar. Meta-analyses show small, inconsistent reductions in fasting blood sugar; it doesn't meaningfully lower HbA1c.

The ingredient groups include vegetables (kale, broccoli, asparagus, cabbage, Brussels sprouts), fruits (berries, acerola cherry, banana, orange), spirulina, turmeric and cinnamon, plus prebiotics and probiotics. With 57 ingredients in one scoop, most are present in very small amounts.

Is TonicGreens Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Talk to your doctor first if you take blood pressure, heart or blood-thinning medication.
  • See a doctor about chest pain, shortness of breath or a persistent cough.
  • Supplements don't replace prescribed treatment.
  • Spirulina from untested sources can be contaminated with toxins; it isn't suitable for people with autoimmune disease or PKU.
  • Turmeric/curcumin supplements may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners and have been linked to rare liver injury.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Cassia cinnamon contains coumarin, which can harm the liver in large regular amounts.
