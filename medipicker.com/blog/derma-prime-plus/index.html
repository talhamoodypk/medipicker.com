What Is Derma Prime Plus?

Derma Prime Plus is a capsule sold for 'normal skin balance' and clearer skin. Its sales page is written by a 'Dr. Ally Ray', introduced as a hepatologist (liver specialist) from Cleveland, Ohio, whose formula focuses on liver-support herbs.

The page mixes reasonable skin-care tips (use sunscreen of SPF 30 or above, clean your phone screen) with a pitch for a liver-cleanse formula. We could not find any public record of a hepatologist by that name.

Derma Prime Plus Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“5 Easy Tips & Tricks That Will Help You Achieve A Normal Skin Balance”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • There's no clinical evidence that liver-cleanse herbs improve acne, dryness or other skin problems.
  • Chanca piedra is studied mainly for kidney stones; artichoke for digestion and cholesterol.
  • The 'doctor' behind the product can't be verified.
  • The page claims restocking would take '8 months' to push larger orders, a common pressure tactic.

Derma Prime Plus Ingredients

The official website names 5 key ingredients in Derma Prime Plus. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Chanca Piedra

Chanca piedra (Phyllanthus niruri), 'stone breaker', is a South American herb traditionally used for kidney stones. A few small studies suggest it may help pass small kidney stones; there is no evidence for skin.

Ginger

Ginger root is a common spice and traditional remedy for nausea. It has good evidence for pregnancy and travel nausea and modest effects on inflammation; no direct evidence for skin conditions.

Burdock Root

Burdock root is a traditional 'blood purifier' herb and a vegetable in Japan. Human evidence is very limited; most claims come from traditional use and lab studies.

Beetroot

Beetroot is rich in nitrates, which the body turns into nitric oxide. Beetroot juice can modestly lower blood pressure and help exercise performance; there's no evidence for skin.

Artichoke Leaf

Artichoke leaf extract is used for indigestion and cholesterol. Small trials show modest relief of indigestion and slight cholesterol reductions.

The sales letter names these herbs 'such as' and doesn't publish a full label or amounts.
