What Is SharpEar?

SharpEar is a once-a-day capsule sold to 'restore your hearing naturally in less than one month', told through the story of the author's wife, Daisy. The sales letter attacks the hearing-aid industry and 'Big Pharma'.

SharpEar Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“Brain Experiment Uncovers the Root Cause of Hearing Loss, And How You Can Restore Your Hearing Naturally in Less Than 1 Month”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • No supplement can restore hearing lost to age or noise, which is caused by loss of inner-ear hair cells.
  • The ginkgo study cited was in sudden hearing loss, a medical emergency that needs prompt steroid treatment, not a supplement.
  • Discouraging hearing aids is harmful: untreated hearing loss is linked to faster cognitive decline, and hearing aids help.
  • St. John's Wort, vinpocetine and huperzine A all carry significant interaction or safety concerns.

SharpEar Ingredients

The official website names 5 key ingredients in SharpEar. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Ginkgo Biloba

Ginkgo leaf extract, usually standardized to 24% flavone glycosides, is one of the most-studied herbal memory supplements. Results are mixed: some trials show small benefits in people with dementia, but the large GEM trial of over 3,000 older adults found it did not prevent dementia or cognitive decline.

St. John's Wort

St. John's Wort is an herb studied mainly for mild to moderate depression. It has reasonable evidence for mild depression, but none for memory or focus.

Bacopa Monnieri

Bacopa is an Ayurvedic herb traditionally used for memory. Several randomized trials found modest improvements in memory recall after 8–12 weeks at about 300 mg of standardized extract a day; it can cause stomach upset.

Vinpocetine

Vinpocetine is a semi-synthetic compound derived from the periwinkle plant (not from seeds). There's no good evidence for hearing or memory.

Huperzine A

Huperzine A is a compound from Chinese club moss that blocks the breakdown of acetylcholine, similar to some prescription Alzheimer's drugs. Chinese studies reported benefits, but a US National Institute on Aging trial found no improvement in Alzheimer's disease at standard doses.

The seller stresses that its ginkgo comes from 'female trees'; there's no evidence that the sex of the tree matters for supplements. Amounts aren't disclosed.

Is SharpEar Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • See a doctor or audiologist about sudden hearing loss, ear pain or new ringing — these need medical attention.
  • Check with your doctor if you take blood thinners, as some plant extracts can affect clotting.
  • Supplements are not a replacement for hearing aids or medical treatment.
  • Ginkgo may increase bleeding risk; avoid combining it with blood thinners or aspirin and stop it before surgery.
  • St. John's Wort interacts with many medicines, including antidepressants, birth control pills, blood thinners, heart and HIV drugs and transplant medicines; it can make them less effective or cause serotonin syndrome.
  • The FDA has warned that vinpocetine may cause miscarriage or harm a developing baby; women who are or could become pregnant should not take it.
  • Huperzine A can slow the heart rate and interact with Alzheimer's medicines and anticholinergic drugs.

Our Verdict on SharpEar

SharpEar's promise to restore hearing is false, and its advice against hearing aids could cause real harm. Sudden hearing loss needs same-day medical attention; gradual hearing loss is best managed with a hearing test and, if needed, hearing aids. Avoid this formula if you take antidepressants, blood thinners or birth control.
