What Is Serenity Prime?

Serenity Prime is a capsule sold to support normal hearing, presented in a letter from 'Adam B. Davis' of Boston and his wife.

Its ingredients (melatonin, chamomile, lemon balm, passionflower, hops, skullcap, ashwagandha, magnesium and St. John's Wort) are a typical sleep and relaxation formula, not a hearing one.

Serenity Prime Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“One Simple Way To Maintain A Normal Hearing”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • None of the ingredients has evidence for hearing.
  • A calming formula might help people sleep despite tinnitus, which is a different benefit from 'normal hearing'.
  • Melatonin can cause next-day drowsiness, so be careful with driving.
  • St. John's Wort interacts with many common medicines.

Serenity Prime Ingredients

The official website names 13 key ingredients in Serenity Prime. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Melatonin

Melatonin is the hormone that signals night-time to the body. It helps with jet lag and can shorten the time to fall asleep slightly; there's no evidence for hearing.

Chamomile

Chamomile is a calming herb usually taken as tea. Small studies suggest it may help mild anxiety and sleep.

Lemon Balm

Lemon balm is a calming herb from the mint family. Small studies suggest it may ease mild anxiety and improve sleep; there's little evidence for digestion.

Passionflower

Passionflower is a calming herb used for anxiety and sleep. A few small trials suggest it may ease mild anxiety; there's no evidence for tinnitus.

Hops

Hops are the flowers used to flavor beer, traditionally used for sleep. Small studies combine hops with valerian for sleep; there's no evidence for nail or skin care.

Skullcap

American skullcap is a traditional calming herb. Human evidence is limited.

Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an Ayurvedic herb classed as an adaptogen. Several small randomized trials found it lowered perceived stress and cortisol and improved sleep at 250–600 mg of root extract a day.

St. John's Wort

St. John's Wort is an herb studied mainly for mild to moderate depression. It has reasonable evidence for mild depression, but none for memory or focus.

Magnesium

Magnesium is a mineral involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions, including muscle and nerve function and blood sugar control. Many adults get less than recommended. Supplements can help low magnesium levels, sleep quality in some studies and, in larger doses, constipation.

Taurine

Taurine is an amino acid found in high amounts in the retina. It's essential for retinal health in animals, but there's no evidence that supplements improve vision in people who eat a normal diet.

Goji Berry

Goji (wolfberry) is an antioxidant-rich berry. Small studies explore it for eye health and well-being; there's no evidence for hearing.

Calcium

Calcium is the main mineral in teeth and bones. Adequate calcium matters for bones; supplements don't remineralize enamel the way topical fluoride does.

Vitamin B6

Vitamin B6 is involved in protein metabolism and making neurotransmitters. Most people get enough from food; very high doses over time can cause nerve damage.

The letter names these 'such as'; amounts aren't disclosed.

Is Serenity Prime Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • See a doctor or audiologist about sudden hearing loss, ear pain or new ringing — these need medical attention.
  • Check with your doctor if you take blood thinners, as some plant extracts can affect clotting.
  • Supplements are not a replacement for hearing aids or medical treatment.
  • Melatonin can cause next-day drowsiness and interacts with sedatives and blood thinners.
  • Chamomile can cause allergic reactions in people sensitive to the daisy family.
  • Skullcap products have been linked to liver injury, sometimes because of adulteration with germander.
  • Ashwagandha isn't recommended in pregnancy, with thyroid medication or for people with autoimmune conditions; rare cases of liver injury have been reported.
  • St. John's Wort interacts with many medicines, including antidepressants, birth control pills, blood thinners, heart and HIV drugs and transplant medicines; it can make them less effective or cause serotonin syndrome.
  • Goji may increase the effect of warfarin.
