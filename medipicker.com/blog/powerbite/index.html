What Is PowerBite?

PowerBite Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“The 'Mineral Candy' That Supports Healthy Teeth And Gums”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Xylitol is the best-supported ingredient: frequent xylitol gum or mints can reduce cavity-causing bacteria, usually at 5–10 g a day spread through the day.
  • One candy a night is unlikely to deliver that much xylitol.
  • Calcium carbonate doesn't rebuild enamel; fluoride and hydroxyapatite do the most for early decay.
  • Dissolving a tablet after brushing at night leaves residue on teeth, so check the label for sugar.

PowerBite Ingredients

The official website names 7 key ingredients in PowerBite. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Calcium Carbonate

Calcium carbonate (chalk) is a mild abrasive used in many toothpastes. It helps remove plaque and surface stains; it doesn't remineralize enamel the way fluoride or hydroxyapatite can.

Xylitol

Xylitol is a sugar alcohol that cavity-causing bacteria can't use for energy. Chewing xylitol gum several times a day (around 5–10 g in total) can reduce cavity-causing bacteria and plaque.

Clove Bud Oil

Clove oil is rich in eugenol, a numbing and antiseptic compound. It shows antifungal activity in lab tests; clinical evidence on nails is lacking.

Myrrh

Myrrh is a resin from Commiphora trees used in traditional medicine and mouthwashes. There is no clinical evidence that myrrh improves focus or mental clarity.

Lysozyme

Lysozyme is an antibacterial enzyme naturally found in saliva and egg whites. It's part of the mouth's natural defences; evidence for supplements is limited.

Wild Mint

Mint provides flavor and a fresh-breath feeling. It freshens breath temporarily; it doesn't treat gum disease.

Mediterranean Sea Salt

Salt has long been used in mouth rinses. Salt-water rinses can soothe sore gums; there's no evidence salt strengthens teeth.

Amounts aren't disclosed. PowerBite doesn't contain fluoride or hydroxyapatite.

Is PowerBite Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Keep brushing, flossing and visiting your dentist — supplements don't replace dental care.
  • See a dentist about tooth pain, bleeding gums or swelling.
  • Products containing xylitol must be kept away from dogs.
  • Xylitol is extremely toxic to dogs, so keep xylitol products away from pets; large amounts can cause diarrhea in people.
