What Is FitSpresso?

FitSpresso is sold as a 'coffee loophole' for weight loss: a capsule you take alongside your morning coffee. The official page is a video sales letter, and the product details only appear inside the video and on the checkout page.

The research list under the video is all about green coffee bean extract, green tea catechins (EGCG), caffeine, L-theanine and L-carnitine, which suggests these are the active ingredients, but the page itself doesn't confirm a supplement facts label in text.

FitSpresso Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“This Special Coffee Loophole Helped Me Drop 61 LBS In Weeks”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • 'Drop 61 lbs in weeks' is an individual testimonial, not a typical result; no trial of FitSpresso exists.
  • Green tea catechins with caffeine raised 24-hour energy use in small studies, but the extra calories burned are small (roughly 100 a day at best).
  • A 2019 meta-analysis of L-carnitine found an average extra loss of about 1.3 kg compared with placebo, which is a modest effect.
  • Green coffee bean extract trials are small and several were poorly run; one widely cited study was retracted.

FitSpresso Ingredients

The FitSpresso page doesn't list its ingredients in text; the studies it cites cover green coffee bean extract, green tea extract, caffeine, L-theanine and L-carnitine. If you take other caffeine products or are sensitive to stimulants, check the label before you order.

Is FitSpresso Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Some weight management formulas contain caffeine or green tea extract — avoid taking them late in the day if you're sensitive.
  • Talk to your doctor first if you take medication for diabetes, blood pressure or your heart.
  • Don't use weight-loss supplements while pregnant or breastfeeding.

Our Verdict on FitSpresso

FitSpresso's likely ingredients (green tea, green coffee, caffeine, L-carnitine) have some research behind them, but the effects in studies are small and nowhere near '61 lbs in weeks'. Because the sales page hides both the label and the price until checkout, read both carefully before you buy, and count its caffeine together with your coffee.
