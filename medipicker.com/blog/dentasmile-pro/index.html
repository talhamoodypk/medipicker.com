What Is DentaSmile Pro?

DentaSmile Pro is sold as a 'dental elixir' to rebuild teeth and gums. The website describes a proprietary formula of eight antioxidants, minerals, vitamins and herbs.

The six ingredients it names (chromium, inulin, berberine, cinnamon, bitter melon and banaba) are a typical blood sugar formula. The website itself explains several of them as 'supporting healthy blood sugar levels'. None is a recognized dental ingredient such as fluoride, xylitol or a studied oral probiotic.

DentaSmile Pro Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“How To Rebuild Your Teeth & Gums TWICE AS FAST With This Powerful "Dental Elixir"”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • Lost tooth enamel and receded gums cannot be 'rebuilt' by any supplement; enamel does not grow back.
  • There is a real link between poorly controlled diabetes and gum disease, but that is managed with diabetes care and dental treatment, not a capsule.
  • Berberine has some lab evidence against oral bacteria, but no clinical trials in capsules for gum health.
  • The product is described as 'scientifically proven' without citing a single study.

DentaSmile Pro Ingredients

The official website names 6 key ingredients in DentaSmile Pro. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Chromium

Chromium is a trace mineral involved in insulin action. Meta-analyses show at most small effects on blood sugar and weight; deficiency is rare.

Inulin

Inulin is a prebiotic fiber, usually from chicory root, that feeds gut bacteria. It can increase bifidobacteria and soften stools at several grams a day; there's no evidence it improves gum health.

Berberine

Berberine is a yellow compound found in barberry, goldenseal and Chinese goldthread. Meta-analyses of randomized trials show it lowers fasting blood sugar, HbA1c and LDL cholesterol, usually at 900–1,500 mg a day split into doses; it often causes stomach upset.

Cinnamon

Cinnamon bark (usually cassia) is a common spice studied for blood sugar. Meta-analyses show small, inconsistent reductions in fasting blood sugar; it doesn't meaningfully lower HbA1c.

Bitter Melon

Bitter melon (Momordica charantia) is a vegetable used in Asian traditional medicine for diabetes. Trials show small and inconsistent blood sugar reductions; evidence quality is low.

Banaba Leaf

Banaba leaf contains corosolic acid, studied for blood sugar. A few small trials found modest reductions in blood sugar; larger studies are lacking.

Six of the eight ingredients are named on the website; amounts are not disclosed.

Is DentaSmile Pro Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • Keep brushing, flossing and visiting your dentist — supplements don't replace dental care.
  • See a dentist about tooth pain, bleeding gums or swelling.
  • Products containing xylitol must be kept away from dogs.
  • Inulin and other prebiotic fibers commonly cause gas and bloating, especially at first.
  • Berberine can add to the effect of diabetes medicine and interacts with drugs cleared by the liver (such as cyclosporine); avoid it in pregnancy.
  • Cassia cinnamon contains coumarin, which can harm the liver in large regular amounts.
  • Bitter melon can lower blood sugar further in people on diabetes medicine and isn't safe in pregnancy.

Our Verdict on DentaSmile Pro

DentaSmile Pro sells a blood sugar formula as a way to rebuild teeth, a promise no supplement can keep. If you have sensitive teeth, bleeding gums or receding gums, a dentist visit, fluoride toothpaste and daily flossing do far more. People with diabetes should also be careful, because berberine, bitter melon and banaba can lower blood sugar alongside their medicine.
