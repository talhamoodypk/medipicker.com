What Is Audifort?

Audifort is a liquid supplement sold to support and 'shield' hearing health. It is taken as a dropper under the tongue or in a drink.

Audifort Claims vs. Evidence

The official website leads with this headline:

“Take care of your hearing starting today”

Here is how the main claims hold up against published research and the information the seller actually provides:

  • None of Audifort's ingredients has clinical evidence for improving hearing or tinnitus.
  • Hearing loss from age or noise damage involves loss of inner-ear hair cells, which no supplement has been shown to restore.
  • Grape seed and green tea antioxidants have been studied for ear protection only in animals.
  • The references cited are general reviews (such as B vitamins and the brain), not studies of the product.

Audifort Ingredients

The official website names 6 key ingredients in Audifort. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Maca Root

Maca is a Peruvian root vegetable sold for energy and libido. Small trials suggest it may improve libido slightly; there is no evidence for hearing.

Grape Seed Extract

Grape seed extract is rich in antioxidant proanthocyanidins. It may modestly lower blood pressure; protection against hearing loss has only been shown in animal studies.

Green Tea Extract

Green tea extract supplies catechins, mainly EGCG, and usually some caffeine. Studies show small effects on energy expenditure and fat oxidation, especially combined with caffeine; weight loss in trials is modest (around 1 kg).

Capsicum Annuum (Chili Pepper)

Capsicum is chili pepper extract, a source of capsaicin. Capsaicin has small effects on metabolism and appetite; there's no evidence it helps hearing.

Gymnema Sylvestre

Gymnema is an Ayurvedic herb known as 'sugar destroyer' because it blocks the taste of sweetness. Small trials suggest modest reductions in blood sugar in people with diabetes; quality is low.

GABA

GABA is the brain's main calming neurotransmitter, sold as a supplement for relaxation. Very little oral GABA is thought to reach the brain; evidence for stress, sleep or tinnitus is weak.

The website lists these as the main ingredients and offers a label image; amounts are not given in text. Several of them (gymnema, green tea and capsicum) are more typical of blood sugar or weight formulas than hearing products.

Is Audifort Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • See a doctor or audiologist about sudden hearing loss, ear pain or new ringing — these need medical attention.
  • Check with your doctor if you take blood thinners, as some plant extracts can affect clotting.
  • Supplements are not a replacement for hearing aids or medical treatment.
  • Concentrated green tea extract has been linked to rare cases of liver injury, especially when taken on an empty stomach; it also contains caffeine.
  • Gymnema can lower blood sugar and may add to the effect of diabetes medicine.

Our Verdict on Audifort

Audifort's ingredients don't match its hearing promise, and no supplement has been shown to reverse hearing loss. If you notice hearing changes or ringing in the ears, see a doctor or audiologist; earwax, infections and medication side effects are treatable, and hearing aids help a great deal. If you try Audifort, the 90-day guarantee gives you time to ask for a refund.
