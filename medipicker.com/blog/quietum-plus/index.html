What Is Quietum Plus?

Quietum Plus is one of the best-known 'ear health' supplements, sold to support hearing and a 'quiet mind' for people bothered by ringing in the ears.

Its ingredients are a male-libido formula: mucuna, maca, epimedium (horny goat weed), tribulus, muira puama, catuaba, damiana, L-arginine and L-tyrosine, plus ashwagandha, ginger, piperine and vitamins. None is a recognized hearing ingredient.

Quietum Plus Ingredients

The official website names 13 key ingredients in Quietum Plus. Here is what each one is and what research says about it.

Mucuna Pruriens

Mucuna pruriens (velvet bean) is a natural source of L-dopa, the precursor of dopamine used to treat Parkinson's disease. It has been studied in Parkinson's disease under medical supervision; there's no evidence for tinnitus.

Maca Root

Maca is a Peruvian root vegetable sold for energy and libido. Small trials suggest it may improve libido slightly; there is no evidence for hearing.

Horny Goat Weed (Epimedium)

Horny goat weed contains icariin, a compound that weakly blocks the PDE5 enzyme in lab studies. There are almost no good human trials on erections; lab potency is far weaker than prescription drugs.

Tribulus Terrestris

Tribulus is a plant widely sold for testosterone and libido. Multiple trials found it does not raise testosterone in men; a few small studies suggest slight improvements in sexual-function scores.

Muira Puama

Muira puama is a Brazilian bark traditionally used as an aphrodisiac. Human evidence is limited to a few old, low-quality studies.

Catuaba

Catuaba is a Brazilian bark traditionally used as an aphrodisiac. There are no good human trials for sexual function or prostate health.

Damiana

Damiana is a Mexican shrub traditionally used as an aphrodisiac. Human evidence is very limited; there are no prostate trials.

Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an Ayurvedic herb classed as an adaptogen. Several small randomized trials found it lowered perceived stress and cortisol and improved sleep at 250–600 mg of root extract a day.

Ginger

Ginger root is a common spice and traditional remedy for nausea. It has good evidence for pregnancy and travel nausea and modest effects on inflammation; no direct evidence for skin conditions.

Piperine (Black Pepper Extract)

Piperine is the compound that makes black pepper hot; it's added to supplements to increase absorption. It can raise blood levels of curcumin and other compounds, which also means it can raise levels of some medicines.

L-Tyrosine

L-tyrosine is an amino acid the body uses to make dopamine, adrenaline and thyroid hormones. Studies suggest it may help mental performance under acute stress; there is no evidence it improves digestion or bowel movements.

L-Arginine

L-arginine is an amino acid the body uses to make nitric oxide, which widens blood vessels. Studies show small blood-pressure effects; it hasn't been shown to aid weight loss or help hearing.

Zinc

Zinc is an essential mineral found in high concentrations in the prostate. Supplements help if you are deficient, but there is no good evidence that extra zinc improves BPH symptoms; long-term high doses can cause copper deficiency.

The label also lists vitamins A and B. Amounts aren't disclosed. Several of the cited references are animal or computer-modeling studies (for example tribulus in mice and ginger in a computational drug-design study).

Is Quietum Plus Safe? Side Effects to Know

  • See a doctor or audiologist about sudden hearing loss, ear pain or new ringing — these need medical attention.
  • Check with your doctor if you take blood thinners, as some plant extracts can affect clotting.
  • Supplements are not a replacement for hearing aids or medical treatment.
  • Mucuna contains L-dopa, which interacts with antidepressants (MAO inhibitors), blood pressure and psychiatric medicines and can cause nausea, low blood pressure and involuntary movements.
  • Ashwagandha isn't recommended in pregnancy, with thyroid medication or for people with autoimmune conditions; rare cases of liver injury have been reported.
  • Piperine can increase the absorption of certain medicines, raising their effects.
  • L-tyrosine can interact with thyroid medication and MAO-inhibitor antidepressants.
  • L-arginine may lower blood pressure and isn't recommended after a heart attack.

Our Verdict on Quietum Plus

Quietum Plus doesn't contain anything that supports hearing. If you have tinnitus or hearing loss, see an audiologist; hearing aids, sound therapy and tinnitus-focused CBT have real evidence. People on antidepressants, Parkinson's or blood pressure medicines should avoid mucuna-based products.
