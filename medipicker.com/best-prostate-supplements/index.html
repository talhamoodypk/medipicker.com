What to Look For in a Prostate Supplement

The ingredient list matters more than the brand. Here is what clinical research says about the ingredients you'll see on prostate supplement labels:

IngredientStudied doseEvidenceWhat studies found
Beta-sitosterol (plant sterols)60–195 mg a dayBetterCochrane review: improved symptom scores and peak urine flow (about 4 mL/s) versus placebo.
Pygeum (African plum bark)75–200 mg extractModerateOlder trials: less night-time urination and better flow; studies small and short.
Pumpkin seed / seed oil320 mg oil to 10 g seedsModerateModest symptom improvement over a year in BPH trials; prostate size unchanged.
Rye grass pollen (Cernilton)Studied extractsSomeSmall trials suggest symptom relief; not in any product above.
Stinging nettle rootVariesLimitedMixed results, often studied combined with saw palmetto.
Lycopene15 mg a dayLimitedOne small trial slowed PSA rise; little urinary-flow data.
Saw palmetto320 mg a dayNo benefitLarge trials (STEP 2006, CAMUS 2011) found it no better than placebo, even at triple doses.
  • Choose a product that publishes its full supplement facts label with doses, not a proprietary blend.
  • Prefer beta-sitosterol, pygeum or pumpkin seed over saw palmetto.
  • Look for a refund window long enough to judge results: at least 60 days, ideally 90 or more.
  • Avoid formulas that promise to shrink your prostate or end night-time urination; no supplement has shown that.

See a Doctor First

About half of men in their 50s and most men over 70 have some prostate enlargement (BPH), but the same symptoms can come from infection, diabetes, medicines or prostate cancer. A doctor can check your prostate, urine and PSA. Prescription alpha-blockers such as tamsulosin often improve flow within days, and finasteride shrinks the prostate over months. Both work better than any supplement.

  • Blood in your urine or semen
  • Pain or burning when urinating, or fever
  • Sudden inability to urinate: get urgent care
  • Symptoms that worsen quickly or wake you several times a night

Prostate Supplement FAQ

What is the best prostate supplement?

Of the prostate supplements we've reviewed, TitanFlow scores highest (3.5 out of 5) because it publishes its full label and is built on pumpkin seed oil and pygeum rather than saw palmetto. No supplement matches prescription treatment, so get checked by a doctor first.

Does saw palmetto work for an enlarged prostate?

Large, well-run trials (STEP and CAMUS) found saw palmetto no better than placebo for urinary symptoms of BPH, even at high doses. Most prostate supplements still use it as their main ingredient.

Which ingredients have the best evidence?

Beta-sitosterol has the most consistent evidence, followed by pygeum and pumpkin seed. Effects are modest: smaller than prescription alpha-blockers such as tamsulosin.

Can a supplement shrink the prostate?

No supplement has been shown to shrink the prostate meaningfully. Prescription 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) do, over 6 to 12 months.

How long do prostate supplements take to work?

Trials of pumpkin seed, pygeum and beta-sitosterol ran for 1 to 12 months. Give any supplement 8 to 12 weeks and track your symptoms before deciding.

Is it safe to take a prostate supplement with tamsulosin or finasteride?

Serious interactions are uncommon, but ask your doctor before combining them. Finasteride changes PSA results, so your doctor needs to know everything you take.

When should I see a doctor instead?

See a doctor before trying any supplement, and urgently if you have blood in your urine, pain, fever, or can't pass urine. The same symptoms can come from infection, diabetes or prostate cancer.
